About this atlas

Bats of the Palearctic brings together bat records from citizen science platforms and biodiversity databases in one map, so that the known distribution of each species can be seen – and updated – in one place.

Where the records come from

  • iNaturalist – including the project Bats of the Palearctic
  • GBIF – museum collections, national databases and other datasets. Records that GBIF received from iNaturalist or Observation.org are shown only once.

GBIF data come from the download 0037651-260928105237408. Please cite GBIF data with the DOI given on that page.

How the map works

Every record is drawn as a circle with the radius of its reported location accuracy: a record accurate to 100 m becomes a 100 m circle when you zoom in. Solid dots are records without a reported accuracy. Locations are shown exactly as published by the source. Platforms such as iNaturalist and Observation.org already generalise the locations of sensitive records, and in those cases the circle covers the generalised area.

Which records are included

The atlas covers the Palearctic as defined by the WWF terrestrial ecoregions of Olson et al. (2001). Records are checked automatically for duplicates, unusual locations and species not yet known from the region, and reviewed by hand. Records found to be wrong are removed. iNaturalist records of the quality grades Research grade and Needs ID are shown; you can filter them on the map. Casual observations are not included.

Using the data

Each record keeps the licence of its source. Photos are shown under their Creative Commons licence with credit to the photographer. For scientific use, please go to the original record via its link and cite the source.

How to cite this atlas

Pestalozzi, M. (2026). Bats of the Palearctic. Version 2026-10-29.

When you use records from the atlas, please also cite the original sources (see above).

Privacy

This site sets no cookies and uses no tracking or analytics. It is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may log the IP address of visitors. Fonts and the map software are served from this site itself. The background map is loaded from Esri (ArcGIS Online), and species photos and range maps from iNaturalist. These services receive your IP address when your browser loads them. A drawn map area is kept only in your browser tab.
